# Family fun, fan recognition and community service

**Status:** brainstorming draft, 2026-10-13. **Latest owner choice: fan voting chooses the
winner.** Staff review eligibility, permissions and safety; valid free fan ballots determine
the award. Ballot implementation, rules, audience and launch remain unapproved proposals.
The owner clarified that fan recognition should
include any eligible account holder submitting a fun game-day video, with Fan of the Game,
Week, Month and Year and awesome-fan badges. The owner asks to continue brainstorming.
This document proposes experiences and operating rules; it does not announce a contest,
award, service campaign, implemented badge or payment opportunity.

**Purpose:** give fans, families and friends enjoyable reasons to participate, tell permitted
athlete stories and return together. Optional donations help explain support for real sports
costs once the separate money requirements are met. A useful fan can watch, create, encourage
or serve without spending.

## 1. Three ways to start

| Approach | What fans experience | Trade-off |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Owner-selected: reviewed entries, free fan voting** | A weekly theme and eligible permitted fan entries; fans choose the award by valid free ballots | Direct community choice; private review/public candidate consent, reliable ballots, closure and abuse handling are prerequisites |
| Curated weekly showcase | Staff curate permitted stories and fan moments without announcing a voted winner | Useful for rehearsal or a noncompetitive activity; it does not substitute a staff-selected winner for the owner's voting choice |
| Ranking by existing views, likes or hype | Existing accumulated engagement determines a result | Rewards existing reach and spam and lacks an award-specific trustworthy ballot; do not use existing counters to select these winners |

Build a small repeatable weekly experience first. Fan awards, school standings and cash
opportunities are separate capabilities with separate decisions. Existing uploads, school
polls or athlete Spotlight do not establish that those capabilities are ready.

## 2. A simple fan journey

**Discover a moment → join a free activity → share something permitted → receive optional
recognition → return for next week's theme.** An approved family story may also explain an
optional donation, without changing the activity or recognition result.

Proposed invitation: **“Show your team spirit. Share a moment, cheer someone on, or help your
community.”** Offer one invitation at a time, a short example, clear audience and a Skip option.
Avoid another mandatory onboarding form or forcing users to become athletes or payees.

| Person | First enjoyable action | Reason to return |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fan or friend | Watch a fan moment; choose a preset positive cheer | New themes, local recaps and opted-in contribution recognition |
| Family member | Watch a Behind the Jersey story; optionally share an approved clip | Follow an athlete's progress and contribute a permitted memory |
| Casual uploader or repeat filmer | Submit an original or properly permitted fan moment | Feedback on review status, accurate credit and noncash recognition |
| Athlete | Join the fun as a fan or tell an optional short story | Community encouragement without money/performance pressure |
| Teen account holder | Join permitted activities with age-appropriate audience choices | Recognition that does not require a public account, payment setup or exposing private life |
| Donor | Read an approved sports-cost explanation after enjoying a story | Understand the family journey; support is voluntary and separate from awards |
| School/community partner | Invite its community to an agreed theme | Inclusive shared participation and recaps; fair standings only after a separate scoring design |

Segments overlap. “Any account” means any account eligible for the particular action under
the accepted age, safety and regional rules. Current local MINOR design closes under-13
accounts. Viewing, entering, publishing identifiable footage, receiving money and acting for
a school require different checks. Public promotion is optional; declining it preserves other
permitted participation. Do not publicly label a person's age or guardian relationship.

## 3. Eight activities people can enjoy

| Activity | Example invitation | Growth loop and contribution |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Fan Moment** | “Show a creative cheer, homemade sign, respectful celebration or family team-spirit moment.” | Free reviewed entries; watch a recap, appreciate others and return for a new theme. No dangerous stunts or appearance judging |
| **Behind the Jersey** | “What do you enjoy about your sport, and what are you learning?” | Short athlete/family stories help friends understand the journey; optional real-cost support is a separate step |
| **Serve Together** | “Choose one small way to help: tidy a shared space, help with permission, or encourage someone.” | Anyone eligible may join; an optional nonidentifying reflection can inspire another person. Watching/learning is a valid alternative |
| **Moments that Matter** | “Find a moment of teamwork, perseverance or kindness.” | Curated existing permitted clips introduce fans to more people and sports |
| **Family Team-Spirit Day** | “Share a family chant, sign-making moment or permitted sports memory.” | Friends invite friends through an approved share; quiet/captioned participation also counts |
| **Community Bingo** | “Watch a new sport, save a useful clip, welcome someone or learn a safe skill.” | Optional personal activity choices; no spending square, public streak pressure or punishment for missed days |
| **Schools Show Up** | “Help your opted-in school community complete this week's shared participation goal.” | Cooperative school spirit first; later comparable school groups can compete under verified free-participation rules |
| **Sunday Recap / Next Theme** | “Enjoy this week's permitted moments; choose Teamwork or Community Service for next week.” | Gives contributors closure and a reason to return; trustworthy voting is a future requirement |

**Recommended first three:** Fan Moment, Behind the Jersey and Serve Together. Moments that
Matter and the recap package their permitted content. Trusted fan voting is part of the
selected award design and must be verified before a winner is announced. Bingo, recorded
badges and school scoring can follow as separate capabilities. A watch-only visitor still
belongs; posting, service, donation and public recognition are optional.

**A sample week:** Monday theme; Tuesday athlete story; Wednesday optional learning/service;
Thursday fan moment; Friday game-day celebration; Saturday optional family contribution;
Sunday recap. One optional invitation per day, with captions, readable controls and no
requirement to complete the calendar. A staff plan does not schedule notifications.

## 4. Fan of the Game, Week, Month and Year

Recognize **what someone contributes**: creative team spirit, respectful encouragement,
welcoming others, teamwork or community help. Supporters, athletes, filmers, parents and
friends may all be fans. No athlete badge, professional filming status, donation or payout
setup should be required for ordinary permitted entry.
For the first version, recognize the entrant's own stated fan contribution. A group video
can include others with permission; it does not automatically nominate every visible or
tagged person. Record creator/subject credit accurately: uploading a clip alone does not
prove the entrant filmed it or owns every person's publicity rights.

| Recognition | Proposed selection | How newcomers stay included |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fan of the Game | Most valid free ballots among eligible reviewed event entries within the agreed award window | Free direct entry; no need to have won before |
| Fan of the Week | A separate weekly ballot among eligible reviewed entries | Rotate themes and provide an accessible entry alternative |
| Fan of the Month | A separate monthly ballot under published entry/window rules | Accept direct monthly entries; earlier wins do not automatically qualify or add votes |
| Fan of the Year | A separate seasonal ballot with bounded eligible candidate material | Accept direct seasonal entries; no lifetime uploads, accumulated views, money or prior-award qualification |

Staff apply published eligibility, theme, rights and safety rules; they do not choose the
voted winner. Invite fans to consider **creative expression, respectful/inclusive conduct,
and community contribution**. Publish the ballot method, entry/vote windows, limits, eligible
regions/ages, visibility, ties/no-valid-result outcome, reviewer conflicts and correction/
appeal process. Honor good sportsmanship across both schools. A family connection to a
reviewer requires recusal. Start with symbolic recognition only; no cash/prize entitlement
is proposed. Disqualification uses a documented rule and appeal, not staff preference.

The owner's choice makes fan voting the award method; a duplicate staff-selected award is
not the starter design. Count valid free ballots for this award, not existing views/hype or
extra points for donations, athlete stats, follower counts, appearance or family hardship.
The candidate rules and voting invitation emphasize positive conduct. Never require obstructing a game,
filming strangers without permission, entering restricted areas or performing unsafe acts.
Check venue filming/access restrictions and rights in official footage, music and school/
event marks. Set an event-posting delay appropriate to the audience; remove location metadata
and avoid real-time minor whereabouts, private schedules and restricted-area details.

Use private review before publication and separate opt-in for public recognition, subjects,
audience and promotional reuse. A minor's public appearance needs the accepted guardian and
subject process; an account link or Stripe verification does not supply that permission.
Allow private acknowledgment or declining promotion. Only entries with accepted public
candidate permission join audience voting; private acknowledgment is not a competing
staff-selected winner of that same award. Existing athlete-only Spotlight must
not be relabeled as a generic fan award.

## 5. Awesome-fan badges

Proposed badges: **Good Sport, Creative Fan, Respectful Filmer, Community Helper, Story
Supporter, Team-Spirit Builder and Welcoming Fan**. Define the behavior, source, eligibility,
reviewer, limits, expiry/correction and visibility before implementation.

Start with a few attainable acknowledgments; let people choose which accepted badges are
visible. Newcomers can participate immediately. Badges are noncash recognition, not a wallet,
currency, tax receipt or claim to payment. They never buy reach, votes, access to a minor,
donation advantages or payout eligibility. Do not imply new fan badges are already supported
by the existing filmer Aura/Props system. Do not sell an award or tie a badge to donation size.

Avoid badge requirements based on uninterrupted attendance, ability to travel, expensive
equipment or exposing family/service beneficiaries. Offer equivalent accessible choices.
Keep award counts and private participation separate from public profile disclosure.

## 6. Service and “once they start earning”

The owner confirmed **both, with separate paths**. Keep these explicit in the design:

- **Recognition path:** after an activity or badge, offer “Want to give back?” with optional
  safe ideas. People can serve before earning anything; the invitation is encouragement.
- **Money path:** if a future approved earning model exists, offer the same optional
  invitation separately from checkout, payout and recipient verification. Do not withhold
  earnings, require service, demand a public post or require donating back.

Within the money path, distinguish existing eligible family donations from proposed creator
opportunities. A fan badge does not make its holder a family donation recipient or a verified
payee. Future options could include permitted clip licensing or clearly disclosed sponsored
creator work, starting with eligible adults and an explicitly approved model. Youth creator
work needs its own age, guardian, contract, school/association, labor, tax and processor review.
Do not promise cash for every upload, a service-reward pool, or a generic fan donation wallet.

Examples: help make a team sign with permission, encourage a new participant, assist an
approved community cleanup safely, share a useful lesson, or reflect on kindness. No app
arranged adult–minor meetups, private contact exchange, unsafe work or mandatory physical
challenge. School/charity branding and organized service activities need the appropriate
partner review. Watching an example or choosing a takeaway is also participation.

An optional reflection can be a short caption, preset takeaway or nonidentifying video.
Review subject rights; never ask for beneficiary names, faces, medical information, a photo
of someone in need, exact location, schedules or location proof. Do not claim verified hours
or charitable impact from a self-reported post. Current photo stories require an athlete
selection; do not tag an unrelated athlete to bypass it. New reflection formats need their
own implementation and data review.

If a sponsor pays for an entry, service post or prize, design a separate commercial feature
with disclosure, child-work/NIL/contest/tax and payment review for the actual jurisdiction.
It is not authorized by noncash fan recognition or inferred from “earning.”

## 7. Athlete stories and voluntary donations

Use three short optional prompts: **“What do you love about your sport?” “What small step are
you proud of?” “Who helped you along the way?”** A family can separately explain a real
sports cost under the approved Rally rules. Do not ask young people to disclose trauma or
poverty, prove gratitude, promise recruiting results or post increasingly personal stories.

Proposed flow: **Watch story → choose a free cheer/follow/share → optionally review a family
cost goal → review recipient, purpose, amount, platform share and applicable refund/eligibility
terms → Donate if permitted.** Preset thanks have no private replies. Donation never changes
rank, recognition, access to a young person or video reach. Donations to families must not be
presented as tax-deductible charity without a confirmed qualifying model.

Separate public story permission from payment consent and reuse rights. Preview what will be
public, who is identifiable, and where it may be promoted. Explain public copying and the
practical removal process without promising retrieval of every third-party copy. Account,
guardian, school affiliation and Stripe status each establish only their stated purpose.

Stories may make support understandable; increased donations are a hypothesis to measure,
not a promise. Live money, trusted settlement, refunds, guardian/payee rules and store policy
remain separate open gates. No new fundraiser is launched by this draft.

## 8. Friendly school competition

Begin with a cooperative theme or shared goal and curated permitted stories. Use opted-in
school identities and authorized marks; a student, uploader or roster confirmer cannot grant
all school rights. Official school standing is a later feature.

Proposed future score: **unique eligible opt-in participants completing a qualifying free
activity ÷ the frozen eligible opt-in cohort**. Label it opt-in participation rather than
whole-school turnout. Compare similar cohort sizes; suppress small identifying groups and
use unranked recognition when evidence is too limited. Define the denominator and threshold
with Data/Privacy and school partners before testing. Do not collect a full school roster
merely to run a fun pilot.

Count each person once toward completion, cap contributions, enforce windows and retries on
the server, detect duplicate/spam entries, provide appeals and audit corrections. Publish how
multi-school supporters are assigned for the activity so one person cannot inflate both
sides. Purchases, donations and athletic performance never add weight.

Current polls use guest/device-local choices and lack a trusted participant ledger,
idempotent retries and effective server closure. Current school board mixes hype, highlights
and distinct fans; it is not the proposed participation rate. Do not announce fair winners
or promise a working campaign pause from those existing controls.

## 12. Refinement: one welcoming weekly experience

**Status:** second brainstorming pass at the owner's request to keep perfecting the concept.
“Fan Zone” is a proposed working name, not a new approved tab. Refine one weekly entry point
within existing discovery rather than requiring fans to navigate eight separate activities.
The owner chose **fan voting selects the winner**. The ballot rules below are recommended
design details for review; the voting capability is not implemented or launched. Separate
badge/Aura and money paths remain confirmed.

### A. Three invitations, one theme

Example theme: **“Support Across the Sidelines.”**

| Invitation | Small first action | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Join the fun** | Watch an example or enter an eligible fan moment | A homemade sign, a captioned cheer, welcoming a visiting fan or a family tradition |
| **Meet an athlete** | Watch a permitted story and choose a free cheer or follow | “What do you love about this sport?” and “What are you learning this season?” |
| **Serve together** | Choose an optional safe activity or watch an example | Help with an authorized cleanup, learn how to welcome someone or reflect privately on kindness |

Show the theme, a short example and these choices; explain any closing window before entry.
Ordinary watching, saving and permitted sharing are useful actions. Do not put Donate in
the activity completion checklist. A separate approved family cost card may follow a story.
Use accessible captions and quiet/seated formats; attendance at a game is not required for
the weekly, monthly or yearly route. No obligation to post one's face or private family life.

### B. A clear fan-entry journey

1. **Choose the theme/event.** Use an approved event association when entering Fan of the Game;
   otherwise offer a weekly theme entry. A fan's school choice states support, not verified
   enrollment or school authority. Do not collect precise location to prove attendance.
2. **Add the contribution.** Offer a short permitted clip and a simple caption about the
   entrant's own contribution. For broader weekly fun, also propose an accessible caption
   or nonidentifying alternative. General fan entry must not require an unrelated athlete tag.
3. **Preview and choose visibility.** Explain who can review the submission and what public
   showcase/recognition would reveal. Separate publication, recognizable subjects, creator
   credit and promotional reuse. Preserve drafts and choices when going Back.
4. **Submit privately.** Show a receipt and **“In review.”** Public feeds, previews and storage
   must not expose it while pending. Do not route activity submissions through an ordinary
   public upload and describe that as private review.
5. **Get a useful outcome.** Distinguish **“Needs a change,” “Accepted contribution,”** and
   **“Eligible for ballot”** after review; ballot eligibility also requires accepted candidate
   audience permission. After confirmed voting results, show **“Won the fan vote”** or
   **“No award this window.”** Upload/network failure is separate with safe Retry. No award
   is not a content-policy violation or a bad-fan label.
6. **Choose the next action.** Watch the recap, enter a later theme or privately enjoy an
   acknowledgment. Show promotion only for the audience and reuse scope actually permitted.

Offer distinct **Withdraw entry, Remove my published content, Report content,** and **Appeal
a decision** actions. Withdrawing from selection alone must not falsely promise deletion of
an already published clip. A subject or guardian needs an accessible removal channel even
without an app account. Staff handle safety/disqualification reasons privately.
Show the request receipt, case status and responsible escalation owner. Explain effects on
the entry, award, badge, recap and promotion; remove/restrict controlled media and copies
where required. Necessary retained audit/financial evidence follows its accepted purpose
and retention limit, with honest limits on recovering copies outside the platform's control.

### C. Acknowledgment and awards serve different purposes

Propose a private **“Thanks for bringing good energy”** receipt for an accepted contribution.
If new badges are implemented, an attainable acknowledgment can precede competitive awards;
never promise automatic Aura or cash. Tell the entrant what was accepted and why. Recognition
may remain private; it must not force a teen account to become public or expose age/guardians.

Use rotating themes: **Creative Team Spirit, Welcoming Fan, Good Sport,** and **Helpful
Community Story**. A first-time entry can be chosen by fans. An accessible captioned cheer
can enter under the same published eligibility rules as an expensive edited video. Existing
reach, equipment, ability to travel and previous awards do not add ballot weight.
Judge a licensed clip by the entrant's accurately credited contribution, not an unsupported
claim to have filmed it. A group clip does not automatically award every person shown.
Community contribution can be encouragement or welcoming others; it does not require a
service activity. A future optional service badge may recognize a chosen eligible service
action, while other fan recognition remains available without serving or posting about it.

| Selection option | Benefit | Additional requirement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Owner-selected: fan voting selects the winner** | Fans directly choose among eligible reviewed entries with permitted public candidate visibility | Explicit permission rules, server voter eligibility/deduplication/rate limits, retry idempotency, closed windows, abuse review and corrections |
| Unselected alternative: staff-selected award with cheers | Staff choose a contribution award and fans express appreciation | This does not implement the owner's choice; do not silently use it for Fan of the Game |
| Unselected alternative: two separate awards | A reviewed award and a voted award have separate results | Requires a later owner decision and two clear workflows; not the selected starter |

Private entries can receive acknowledgment without public exposure. Audience voting requires
a clearly explained permitted public candidate display; never silently enroll a private clip
in voting. Public display for an entry should not require making the entire account public;
the actual per-entry permission/control design needs verification. If that exposure is
declined, preserve private acknowledgment and other permitted activities. All entry limits,
period rules and public promises need acceptance before a pilot; this draft selects no winner.

**Proposed ballot details to refine next:**

- One eligible candidate entry per account per award window, with revision before the entry
  deadline; this cap is for the award, not ordinary app uploads. Guests may view permitted
  candidates; counted ballots require an eligible signed-in account and applicable age checks.
- One free ballot per eligible account per award window. Propose allowing a voter to change
  their choice until the server deadline; it replaces their ballot rather than adding one.
  Publish whether candidates may vote for themselves; recommend no self-votes for the pilot.
- The eligible entry with the most valid ballots wins. Propose clearly labeled shared winners
  for an exact tie and no award when there are no valid ballots. No random draw, cash prize
  or staff preference resolves a tie. Accept these rules before opening, not after results.
- Confirm receipt only when the trusted service accepts the ballot. Show **Pending, Confirmed,
  Not accepted,** or **Voting closed** accurately. A timeout can require reconciliation;
  safe retries use the same request identity. Offline/late queued votes cannot bypass closure.
- Enforce eligibility, one-ballot storage, revisions, rate limits, idempotency and windows
  on the server. One account is not proof of one unique human. Use proportionate bot/multiple-
  account/abuse checks and appeals; do not require every fan to complete Stripe or submit ID.
- Give eligible candidates comparable presentation and rotate discovery order. Do not sell
  placement or vote weight. Prefer hiding running totals until closure to reduce bandwagon
  effects. Bigger fan networks can still have an advantage; measure concentration without
  promising perfectly equal odds or collecting unnecessary social graphs.
- Make optional shares about the permitted competition, with no buying votes, donation-to-
  vote offers, contact harvesting or spam. Keep entry and ballot actions separate; sharing
  does not add votes. Do not create an adult–minor private messaging channel to solicit votes.
- Close and pause must affect server writes. Pause need not erase legitimate earlier ballots;
  publish whether and how the window resumes. Freeze/reconcile valid totals before results.
  Publish exclusions, invalid-ballot handling, appeals and recount/correction rules; staff
  apply those rules and never replace the winner with their favorite. Protect safety reasons.
  Invalid ballots do not automatically prove entrant misconduct: malicious outsiders can
  target a candidate. Investigate provenance, exclude invalid ballots, permit appeals and
  correct the valid tally without punishing an innocent entrant or exposing private details.
- Publish confirmed recognition with permitted alias/media and opt-in announcement/reuse.
  Do not publish voter identities, teen loser lists or sensitive exclusion information.
  Award-specific tally review and necessary audit retention need Data/Privacy acceptance.

### D. Stories, service and support stay inviting

A story can end with **“Follow the journey,” “Send a preset cheer,”** or an approved **“View
family sports-cost goal.”** No promise of recruiting or better reach. A family can decline a
public story and still retain its otherwise permitted support path.

After recognition or eligible money activity, offer **“Want to give back?”** with **Learn,
Choose an activity, Share if you want,** and **Skip**. The service option is also available to
people who have earned nothing. Do not use service posts as proof for releasing money.
Keep beneficiaries' identities and exact whereabouts out of required evidence. If a future
post is compensated or sponsored, its terms and disclosures use the separately approved
commercial model; an optional reflection cannot silently become paid work.

### E. Make school spirit welcoming

Begin with **“Both communities showed up”** recaps and a shared theme. Recognize respectful
support across school lines. Fan of the Game and a school's participation goal are separate:
one person's award does not prove their school won. Later competition needs the §8 trusted
cohort/scoring contract. Good Sportsmanship recognition can celebrate welcoming opponents;
do not reward taunts, public loser labels or donation totals.

### F. Return gently and learn before expanding

Show an in-app review receipt and a weekly recap. Any future optional notifications need
verified delivery, privacy-safe content, consent/preferences and quiet-time handling. Do not
send repeated “you missed out” alerts or expose a teen's participation on a lock screen.
No scheduling or notification delivery is implemented by this draft.

Proposed pilot sequence: rehearse with permitted samples, test one staffed weekly theme,
then consider voted Game/Week recognition after its admin/permission/ballot workflow is verified.
Choose an accepted submission/handling cap and review promise from measured staffing before
opening entries. Do not manufacture a guaranteed review deadline from unknown capacity.
Month/Year, additional voting formats, recorded badges, school scoring and creator payments have their
own later design choices; they need not all ship together.

Growth/Product bring three observations to the joint review: **did newcomers understand and
complete an enjoyable permitted action; did people return voluntarily; could staff handle it
safely and affordably?** Use minimal accepted measurement and distinguish event attendance,
account activity and unique contributors. QA tests failed/retried entries, pending privacy,
public consent refusal, revoked permissions, awards/appeals, actual stop/removal and accessible
routes. Donation outcomes are assessed separately after money gates. Current baselines,
conversion effects and review throughput remain unknown.

This refinement is discussion-ready product direction, not an approved implementation spec.
Next, refine ballot details and the first pilot audience, then present a focused entry/review/
voting design with the admin contract. Existing charter §11 and team duties already cover
these responsibilities; no new roles, source paths or automatic team activity are introduced.
