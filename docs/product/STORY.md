# Tapua'i — Our story

**Back every athlete.** Not just the star.

This is the source for how we talk about Tapua'i: in marketing, the pitch deck, the App Store page,

## The mission, in one breath

Make donations and highlights easy for families, so they can promote their kid and help them get
recruited. Give kids the chance to share good workouts with their fans (Train Like Me) and to serve
like their favourite player (Serve Like Me). Open an avenue for people to donate toward a young
athlete's sports costs and career growth — and make sure every athlete says thank you.

## Why we exist

Every Friday night there is a kid on the field whose talent is not the question. Money is.

Cleats that fit. The camp fee. Gas to the tournament three towns over. The showcase where the
coaches actually are. The application fees, the visit, the first semester's books. For some
families these are line items. For others they are the reason a dream quietly ends.

And when money does show up in youth sports today, it finds the same few people. NIL deals, in
college and now in high school, flow to the most famous player on the roster. The starting
quarterback gets the car-dealership deal. The lineman who opened the hole, the backup who never
missed a practice, the girl on the volleyball team whose family works two jobs — they get a
shout-out, if that.

Tapua'i exists so that **money is never the reason a young athlete stops**, and so that support
reaches **every athlete, not just the one on the poster**.

*Tapua'i* is Samoan for the quiet, faithful support a family and village give someone who is out
there competing for them. That is the whole idea. The app just gives the village a phone.

That is the arc every Rally story should follow:

1. **A kid with the talent and the drive.**
2. **A real, specific cost standing in the way.**
3. **The community showing up** — family, neighbors, alumni, local businesses, strangers who saw
   the clip.
4. **The cost disappears. The kid keeps going.**
5. **The destination** — the next level, college, or a life they are ready for either way.

## Five promises

### 1. Friday night never ends — watch, tag, compete

The feed is the heartbeat. Fans film the plays, tag the athletes, and hype the moments that
matter. Then the fun starts: who's the most-hyped player this week, the most-viewed Highlight,
the school whose fans showed up loudest. Rivalry week between two schools becomes a week-long
contest in the stands and on the feed. It is friendly competition that keeps athletes wanting to
play well and keeps fans coming back after the final whistle.

*What exists today:* the Highlights feed, fan upload and tagging, Seki / Aura / Flex, weekly
Spotlight standings, rivalry polls. *What's next:* ROADMAP Mission track M1 and M8.

### 2. The Rally — the whole community, one tap

GoFundMe, but built for game day and built for athletes. A Rally is a specific goal on an
athlete's profile ("Club season — $600", "Showcase trip — $350", "Cleats — $120"), a progress bar
the whole feed can see, and a celebration the moment it fills. You give right from the highlight
you just watched, or from the flyer QR on the fence at the game. The athlete thanks you with a
clip, not a form letter.

*What exists today:* donations from a Highlight or profile, Locker gear goals fans donate toward, an
"equipped" moment, gift links and QR flyers — and (built 2026-09-29, going live once the database
change is applied) **Rally goals** on the athlete's profile with a progress bar, confetti when one
fills, and a thank-you with a clip of what it paid for. Athletes **say thanks to every donor** with a
tap. *What's next:* the Rally meter on the Highlights feed (ROADMAP M8).

**How Tapua'i keeps the lights on — said plainly.** 90% of every donation goes to the athlete's family
account. Tapua'i keeps 10% to run the app, including card processing, and the donate sheet says so
before anyone pays. Donors can also add an optional tip for Tapua'i — it starts at "No tip", and it
never comes out of the donation.

### 3. Every athlete — not just the star

The NIL gap is real: a handful of athletes get almost everything. Tapua'i is designed against
that. The feed and Spotlight make room for athletes who have not been backed yet. A fan can back a
whole team, and the gift is shared across the roster. Boosters can match a Rally. The walk-on and
the backup are as findable as the headliner.

*What's next:* ROADMAP Mission track M3.

### 4. Train Like Me. Serve Like Me. — pass it on

The athletes who made it know exactly what it took. **Train Like Me** is them showing the next
generation how they work — the 5 a.m. sessions, the drills, the off-season. **Serve Like Me** is
them showing how they give back — to their family, their school, their community. Fans ask real
questions and get real answers, and one day soon, a real 1-on-1 with an athlete they look up to.

It keeps the young ones inspired and the stars grounded. It is the part of Tapua'i that no one
else has, and the part that makes the name mean something: support flows up to the athlete, and
wisdom flows back down.

*What exists today:* Train Like Me and Serve Like Me as Highlight categories (Serve Like Me earns
2× Aura). *What's next:* ROADMAP Mission track M4 — Ask an athlete with video replies, then
1-on-1 sessions under strict safety rules.

### 5. The Pathway — ready for college, ready for life

Support is not just for this season. Families can set aside part of what the community gives
toward **getting ready for college** — camps, showcases, test prep, application fees, visits — and
toward **life beyond the game**, for the day every athlete eventually faces, pro or not. And the
athlete builds a verified showcase — highlights, a simple game line, measurables — that is theirs
to share with anyone, including a college coach.

*What exists today:* verified Varsity, profile Highlights / Stats / Vault, combine measurables.
*What's next:* ROADMAP Mission track M5 (a light game line from the game manager), M6 (a
showcase link) and M7. The savings side needs counsel and a licensed
partner before it is built (see guardrails).

## Guardrails (these keep the story true and the athletes eligible)

High-school athletes are mostly minors, and state associations treat money differently from the
NCAA. One wrong sentence can cost a kid their eligibility. Full reasoning in
[LEGAL_BRIEFING.md](../legal/LEGAL_BRIEFING.md) §3 and §6.

**Always:**

- Support is a **gift to an athlete and their family**, never payment for a play, a stat, a win
  or a ranking. Rally goals are for **costs** (fees, gear, travel, college prep), not rewards.
- For minors, money goes to a **parent- or guardian-controlled** account, not a kid's wallet.
  Say "to the family's account for the athlete" — not "cash in the kid's pocket".
- The athlete sees every gift, and **no money leaves the family account without the athlete's
  OK**. At 18 (or their state's age) it becomes theirs to move. Say "held for you", not "your
  parent's money".
- Stories of real athletes are published **only with the athlete's and a parent's written
  consent**, and can be taken down on request.
- Say what is real today. Until live keys and counsel sign-off, it is a test.
- Any 1-on-1 between a fan and an athlete follows ROADMAP M4's safety rules: in the app, recorded,
  a parent involved whenever a minor is on either side, no private messages with minors.

**Never:**

- "Get paid for that touchdown." / "Earn per point." / anything tying money to performance.
- "Invest in this athlete." / "Grow your money." / returns, interest, or "fund" language that
  sounds like a security. The Pathway is setting aside gifts, not an investment product, and the
  savings side is not built until counsel and a licensed partner say how.
- "Approved NIL." / "Guaranteed scholarship." / promises about recruiting outcomes.
- "Aura is worth money." Aura is reputation. It is never cash.
- Naming or ranking kids by how much money they received, in public.
- Leaderboards by dollars. Boards rank hype and views, never donations.

**Before any campaign ships:** one written position from the state association (Utah first) that
fan gifts not tied to performance are allowed — ROADMAP Phase 1 item 5.

## Proof we need

The composite above is a placeholder. The real marketing is real families. During the Phase 3
test wave:

1. Pick 3–5 athletes (with parent consent) and run a real Rally for a real cost.
2. Film the moment the goal fills, and the thing it paid for (the camp, the cleats, the trip).
3. Record what changed, in their words.
4. Those become the launch stories. No invented numbers, no borrowed outcomes.
